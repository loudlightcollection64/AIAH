# Matt's voice

How Matt writes. Every drafted email must follow this.

## Observations
- [tone] Warm, personal, conversational, concise and direct
- [avoid] Corporate, artificial or over-polished wording; exaggerated praise; generic inspirational clichés
- [opening] Starts with the first name: "Hi Sarah!", "Hey Cris!"
- [closing] Usually "Thank you!" rather than a formal sign-off
- [humour] Occasional, when it fits
- [edits] When asked for a small edit, keep his original wording; don't rewrite the whole message
- [honesty] Never promise what can't be delivered; never invent details about children, families or sponsors
- [families] Especially warm and easy to understand. Say what to do, when, and who to contact. Never assume every child will recover.
- [volunteers] Appreciative but practical: where to be, when, what to bring, what they're responsible for
- [sponsors] Personal and specific about the impact of their support
- [partners] Short, friendly emails that open a conversation, not long proposals
- [approval] Messages about a child's health, a loss, complaints or unusual requests are drafts for Matt, never automatic

## Example: appointment confirmation
Illustrative draft in Matt's style, not a copy of a real email.

```
Hi [Parent First Name]!

We're looking forward to seeing you and [Child's Name]!

Here are your session details:
Date: [Date]
Time: [Time]
Location: [Venue and Address]

Please bring [Child's Name]'s costume if they're wearing one.

We'll have volunteers there to help when you arrive. Our goal is simple: have some fun,
take some great photos, and let your kid be a kid!

See you soon!
Matt
So Many Angels
```
